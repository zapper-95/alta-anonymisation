An individual, known professionally as Voris, was a renowned fashion designer specializing in suede sportswear, earning a prestigious Neiman Marcus Fashion Award in 1942 for this person’s work. As Voris Marker, the individual also worked as a sculptor. Born in Baker City, Oregon, according to a marriage certificate, but growing up on a ranch near Billings, Montana, this person worked as a copywriter for an advertising agency in Chicago after finishing school. While in Chicago, the person had an operation on their leg, which led to returning home as an invalid. Whilst recuperating, this individual discovered soft leather, inspiring clothing creation. The person made a golf skirt for a Spokane tournament participant. Other players and spectators noticed the skirt and requested leather garments too. This individual founded 'Suedes by Voris' in 1933, and within a few years, had shops in Hollywood and across the Southwestern U.S., selling a wide range of clothing entirely made in suede. The range included day and evening wear, hats, jewelry, and men's jackets, shirts, and ties, made in various colors. One noted design was a gold-plated evening jacket. When Elsa Schiaparelli visited Neiman Marcus, she chose a Voris coat as "the most distinctive fashion seen by an American creator." It is suggested that this inspired awarding the Neiman Marcus Fashion Award in 1942. Voris later dedicated time to sculpting, creating a bronze portrait of Herbert Marcus, the store's co-founder, and a memorial bust of Gary Cooper for the Friars Club of Beverly Hills in 1961. In November 1936, the person married Clifford H. Marker, who became a notable official in the 1960s. This person passed away in May 1973.
