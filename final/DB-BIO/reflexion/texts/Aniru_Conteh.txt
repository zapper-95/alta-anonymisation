A person (born in the early 1940s and died in the early 2000s) was a Sierra Leonean physician and expert on the clinical treatment of a viral haemorrhagic fever endemic to West Africa. This person studied medicine at a university in Nigeria and taught at a teaching hospital there. This person later returned to Sierra Leone, joining an international disease prevention program at a hospital, first as superintendent and then as clinical director. After a civil conflict began in the early 1990s, the program was closed and this person and the medical team moved to a government hospital, where this person spent the next two decades running the only dedicated ward for the fever in the world. This person collaborated with a British charity to promote public health in Sierra Leone through education and awareness campaigns intended to prevent the fever. With little funding and few supplies, this person successfully reduced mortality rates until an accidental needlestick injury led to their own death from the disease. This person received renewed public attention a few years later as the hero of a memoir about their work.
