A person (1893–1981) was a British writer, amateur diplomat and sympathiser of the German regime who was educated at a prestigious school. In the early 1940s, this person travelled to Italy to meet Ulrich von Hassell, a German ambassador. This person believed the ambassador would be receptive to the idea of a pact between Britain and Germany at the outbreak of World War II. The terms of this hypothetical pact were that Germany would have a free hand in Europe in return for Britain managing other regions of the world. However, this person unsuccessfully attempted to arrange a meeting with both General Dwight D. Eisenhower and General Bernard Montgomery. This person had the ear of several members of a British legislative body, but the level of support of said members is unknown. A handwritten note by an intelligence agency stated: "This person went to Italy with the knowledge of the Foreign Office in order to develop contacts. This person greatly exceeded instructions." The intelligence agency decided against having this person arrested, due to the possible support received from members of the legislative body, and the embarrassment this would have caused. This person was on friendly terms with influential members of the British aristocracy, who also were sympathizers. When Winston Churchill succeeded Chamberlain as Prime Minister, a certain figure, Lord Halifax, remained as Foreign Secretary until January 1941, when he was sent to Washington, D.C. as British Ambassador. Consequently, this person's political influence disappeared.
