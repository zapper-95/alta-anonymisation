Graduated from a prestigious art school in the early 1970s, an architect, with colleagues, founded a teaching unit. A few years later, this architect received a "master of city planning in urban design" at an acclaimed university in the U.S., and, upon returning to their home country, hosted a section of urban architecture in a renowned journal. From the mid-1970s to the early 1990s, projects were carried out in collaboration with a partner. In the early 1980s, this architect won a "first prize of the jury" in a competition for a major opera house and then, a few years later, a prize for a significant urban project. In the mid-1980s, alongside colleagues, this architect won the competition for the construction of a famous park in a major city, which took several years to complete, and afterwards, they won an exposition contest for a national pavilion. In the late 1980s, this architect designed The Atrium, a notable headquarters in France. In the early 1990s, this architect won a competition organized by a Public Establishment for redevelopment of a major business district area for the construction of a business complex. Then they realized the headquarters of a major national television network in a major city, as well as many others, such as well-known corporations in various locations.
