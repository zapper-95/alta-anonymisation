A painter (born early 20th century) was born in a beach suburb of a city in the Netherlands. After the usual schooling that prepared this person for the business world, the decision was made to follow an old dream of being an artist. This individual studied for several years at an academy of fine arts and took post-graduate classes in sculpture. In the late 1950s, this artist came to America with a partner and five children. They started painting gigantic faces, foaming glasses of beer, and entire scenes up to several meters long, for the advertising industry. A good year of this was enough and this painter went on their own, doing murals, portraits in oils or bronze, always on commission. A long period of involvement in business followed and painting continued in spare time, without the pressure of having to make a living at it. Now, in retirement, this artist can do as they please and describes their work as follows: I refuse to paint "sure sellers", such as the cute, the corny, the romantic, the sentimental, the narrative. My favorite subject is the contemporary urban scene, which I find exciting and uniquely suited to my taste. I like to use clues that give an illusion of space, or only a suggestion of it around the corner or beyond the hill. The opposites of mass and space, of volumes and voids intrigue me. They are the basis of my compositions. The urban landscape is rich in geometric planes that can be arranged in expressive combinations of color and light-dark values. I want to see each element in my work in harmony with the total. That includes clouds, traffic signs, telephone poles etc. I will change the subject to suit my purpose. Things are moved, added or left out in a process of translating reality into a painted and new reality, that may be subjectively interpreted.
