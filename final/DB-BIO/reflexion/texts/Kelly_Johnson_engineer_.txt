A person (born in the early 20th century) was an American aeronautical and systems engineer. This individual is recognized for contributions to a series of important aircraft designs, most notably the Lockheed U-2 and SR-71 Blackbird. Besides the first production aircraft to exceed Mach 3, this person also produced the first fighter capable of Mach 2, the United States' first operational jet fighter, as well as the first U.S. fighter to exceed 400 mph, and many other contributions to a large number of aircraft. As a member and first team leader of the Lockheed Skunk Works, this person worked for more than several decades and is said to have been an "organizing genius". This individual played a leading role in the design of over forty aircraft, including several honored with a prestigious trophy, acquiring a reputation as one of the most talented and prolific aircraft design engineers in the history of aviation. In the early 21st century, as part of its commemoration of the 100th anniversary of the Wright Brothers' flight, a notable publication ranked this person 8th on its list of the top 100 "most important, most interesting, and most influential people" in the first century of aerospace. A colleague of this person's Lockheed boss, referring to this person's ancestry, once remarked to another: "That person can actually see air." This person also helped to design a particular aircraft a decade before the famous SR-71. This aircraft was a highly successful single engine Mach 4.3 capable Ramjet composed mainly of steel, which was used to test American air defenses against nuclear missiles. The information and experience this individual gained was later used to produce a spy plane for an intelligence agency. This person then used the combined knowledge of previous projects to produce the SR-71 Blackbird.
