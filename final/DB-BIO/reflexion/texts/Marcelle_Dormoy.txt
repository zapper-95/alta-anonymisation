A notable European fashion designer and former model, recognized for her groundbreaking contributions to haute couture, was active from the late 1920s to about 1950. She started out as a mannequin for a prominent couturier, which helped her learn to 'understand the female body.' She worked with the esteemed fashion house Vionnet alongside other designers. As première d'atelier, she was head of the workroom there until leaving to launch her own couture house in the late 1920s. This date was open to question even while she was in business, with some sources stating a business launch in the mid-1930s, and a former mannequin recalling a launch date in the late 1920s in memoirs. In the late 1930s, she had a portrait painted by an artist who accepted a fur coat as payment. This portrait was later sold by a Swiss auction house in the 2010s. She remained with another designer in a coastal city prior to returning to the fashion capital following significant historical events during World War II. Continuing work during the Occupation, she provided clothes to clients such as a notable actress. In early 1946, an international magazine published an article about the actress and this designer, noting that her designs were among the first post-war models 'by leading designers' bought by international buyers for export, despite a poor exchange rate. A picture caption in the article noted her designs typically featured free-flowing lines. The couture house closed around 1950, and she passed away in the mid-1970s. Her heirs presented a late 1940s evening gown designed by her to a prestigious museum.
