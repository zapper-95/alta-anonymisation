A renowned martial artist (1862–1940) was one of Wu Quanyou's three primary disciples in Wu-style t'ai chi ch'uan. When Wu Quanyou's son moved from Beijing to Shanghai in 1928, this martial artist remained to lead the Beijing Wu-style group. This individual was the founder of the Beijing Tai Miao t'ai chi ch'uan Research Centre. In 1929, the first documentary book on Wu-style t'ai chi, 'The record of Wu Style Tai Chi Chuan,' was published by Wu Quanyou's son, this martial artist, and another practitioner. This person's primary disciple was Yang Yuting. The beginnings of Wu-style were created by Wu Quanyou (1834–1902), a Manchurian who studied under Yang Luchan (founder of the Yang style) and Yang Banhou. Wu Quanyou’s son, Wu Jianquan (1870–1942), loved martial arts from a young age and studied under his father. After 1912, Wu Jianquan continuously developed the teaching of t'ai chi ch'uan at the Beijing Sport Research Society, gradually refining his father’s style to what is currently recognized as Wu-style.
