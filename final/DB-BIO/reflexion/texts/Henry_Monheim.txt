A person (1824—1893) was an architect who was one of the first non-Mormon architects practicing in a city in Utah. This person served as the first president of the city's first professional architectural association. This person was associated briefly with other architects in several works credited to them as a group. These works, during just part of the early 1890s, included several buildings which provided entrance into the city for the other architects. Before and after this partnership ended by this person's death in the early 1890s, the other architects became much more well-known and was much more influential.
