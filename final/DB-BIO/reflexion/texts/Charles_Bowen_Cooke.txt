A person (born in the mid-19th century – died in the early 20th century) was born in a village in the formerly known Huntingdonshire and was Chief Mechanical Engineer (CME) of a prominent British railway company. This engineer was the first to add superheating to the locomotives of the railway. This engineer wrote a book called British locomotives: their history, construction; and modern development which was published in the late 19th century, with subsequent editions following. A second book, Developments in Locomotive Practice followed a few years later. Whilst CME of the railway company, this engineer was responsible for the introduction of several new locomotive designs, including notable classes. This engineer was appointed a Commander of the Order of the British Empire in the early 20th century New Year Honours for their efforts during a major conflict. They died in the early 20th century and are buried in a churchyard in a region of Cornwall.
