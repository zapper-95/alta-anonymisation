An American martial artist and pioneer in the early days of mixed martial arts, this person co-founded the Scientifically Aggressive Fighting Technology of America, a self-defense system rooted in San Soo Kung Fu. Together with the co-founder Lew Hicks, they trained under Jerry Peterson, the originator of the S.C.A.R.S. combat system. Both achieved their San Soo Kung Fu black belts in 1993, with Hicks picked to corner this person at a UFC event. This individual's inauguration into mixed martial arts occurred during the Ultimate Fighting Championship 5, swiftly winning via TKO against an opponent. The fight, particularly contentious, resulted in a fine due to two infractions of established rules. Despite defending the actions by referencing the event's promotion as lacking rules, the individual conceded the presence of banned moves. Following the win, a hand injury led to a withdrawal from the tournament. The aftermath involved struggles to secure further fights, despite publicly challenging Royce Gracie, a renowned competitor, allegedly due to blacklisting by a UFC promoter, Art Davie. Attempts at publicizing a comeback via martial arts outlets, specifically in the Inside Kung Fu magazine, led to a match against the notable Vitor Belfort, ultimately ending in a KO loss. This individual has remained inactive in professional fights post this event, though stated the conditions of the matchup were sub-optimal, harboring ambitions of being a 'world champ' under different terms. In April 2010, this person assisted a Team Quest fighter Steven Tobias at a Gladiator Challenge event, achieving victory. Since the late 2000s, they have encountered numerous injuries while striving to regain top form, including breaking three toes and an ankle. By the end of 2014, they maintained a flawless 9-0 record in cornering fighters, comprising 8 KOs and one submission.
