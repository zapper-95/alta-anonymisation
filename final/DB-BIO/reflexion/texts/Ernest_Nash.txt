A person (born in late 19th century and died in mid 20th century) was a student of Roman architecture and a pioneering archaeological photographer. This person was born with a different name but later changed it while living in another country between 1939 and 1952. This person was a graduate of a university in Germany and had a law office in their native city. This person first went to Italy in the 1930s with a goal of documenting important sites in photographs. The main reason for fleeing their native city was the institution of certain racial laws. This person emigrated to the United States but returned to Italy to continue the work. This person established a significant archive at an academic institution in Rome and served as its director. Besides architectural photography, this person also produced a series of portraits of renowned musicians, including a portrait of Béla Bartók whose photo is still used by a major publication. This person is survived by a partner, two descendants, and multiple generations of grandchildren. Previous partners have already passed away. This person died in Rome in the mid 1970s.
