Born in Berkshire County, Massachusetts, Spink moved to Stark County, Ohio, in 1815. This individual taught school for several years in Kendal, Ohio. He was appointed deputy surveyor of Wayne County in October 1815 and served until December 1816. County surveyor from 1816 to 1821, serving also for a time as district surveyor. County auditor in 1820 and 1821. Spink served as a prominent member of the State house of representatives in 1821 and 1822, during which time he contributed significantly to legislative developments. He was employed in the register's office at Wooster from 1822 to 1824. Spink was appointed register by President Monroe in 1824. Reappointed by President Adams in 1828 and served until 1832. He actively engaged in mercantile pursuits in Wooster. Presidential elector in 1844 for Clay/Frelinghuysen. Member of the State board of equalization in 1846. Spink was a delegate to the Whig National Convention in 1852. He was appointed by Governor Chase as one of the directors of the Ohio Penitentiary in 1856. Spink was elected as a Republican Congressman to the Thirty-sixth Congress and served from March 4, 1859, until his death in Wooster, Ohio, on May 31, 1859. He was interred in Wooster Cemetery. Spink was married to Nancy Campbell Beall, daughter of General Reasin Beall, in 1819. They had six children. Spink was a Baptist.
