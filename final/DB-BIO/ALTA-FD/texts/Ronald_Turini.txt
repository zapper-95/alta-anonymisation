Born in Vancouver, this Canadian pianist debuted professionally at the age of twelve with the National Film Board of Canada. At eighteen, the pianist met Arthur Rubinstein, which later led to an apprenticeship with him. The pianist has performed in New York and made an American debut at Lincoln Center in 1960. They returned to Avery Fisher Hall in 1965 and 1970.

In 1958, the pianist performed Brahms' Piano Concerto with the Toronto Symphony and played under Leonard Bernstein in 1959. The following decade, a debut in Philadelphia took place in 1968, and performances in Boston and Montreal occurred. Throughout their career, the pianist has been a soloist with numerous prestigious orchestras, including the Toronto Symphony, Vancouver Symphony, Los Angeles Philharmonic, San Francisco Symphony, Orchestra of St. Luke's, Boston Symphony, Philharmonia Orchestra, and Berlin Philharmonic.

The pianist toured extensively across continents, including several tours in Europe, and performed in cities such as Rotterdam, Brussels, Milan, Budapest, and Stockholm. Asian travels were notable as well. Performances were also part of the Wigmore Hall series, and the pianist captivated audiences in Central American tours.

Praised for thoughtful elegance, grace, and intensity, the pianist's skill is regarded as extraordinary. Recognition includes a review by BBC Music Magazine in 1964, and the particularly prestigious suggestion for a Grammy Award in 1972 for the recording of Bartók's Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion.

In their career, the pianist earned accolades at the Leeds International Piano Competition and the Cleveland International Piano Competition. The pianist was well-liked among staff and students, usually accompanied by fascinating men and showing a preference for vintage European cars. They were a founding member of the North Star Ensemble and held the position of Honorary Professor of piano performance at the University of British Columbia. Until 2007, the pianist remained in Hamilton, Ontario.
