Eleanor was the professional name of an American designer who created silk sportswear and sold clothing entirely made in silk. She designed a wedding dress for a Santa Clara woman, and her range included day and evening wear, hats, jewelry, men's jackets, shirts, and ties. Eleanor crafted a bronze evening jacket and sold a variety of clothing to many entertainers. Coco Chanel selected a coat by Eleanor at a prominent fashion retailer. 

Born in Helena, Montana, Eleanor grew up near a city in Montana. After completing her education in the Central U.S. region, she worked as a journalist for a newspaper in Kansas City. She returned home sick after undergoing leg surgery in a U.S. city. Eleanor, inspired by leather materials, started a fashion business in the 20th century. She had shops in Chicago by the 1940s.

Eleanor received a prominent award during the 20th century. She was also commissioned to create a marble portrait of a notable business figure. Eventually, she left the business to focus on writing. Eleanor worked in the visual arts field and died on the 14th June 1978.
