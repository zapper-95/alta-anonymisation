Emily Langdon, born on 15 April 1941, is an American former tennis player who was active during the 1970s and 1980s. Langdon participated in all four Grand Slam tournaments throughout her career. Her notable achievements include reaching the quarterfinals in singles and doubles at the 1978 French Open. At the Canadian Open, she reached the third round in singles in 1981 and advanced to the fourth round in 1983.

In 1984, Langdon reached the semifinals at the Australian Open. She also reached the second round in singles at the Italian Open in 1982, and the third round in doubles at the same event. Her doubles performance also includes reaching the second round at the 1979 French Open.

During the early 1980s, Langdon participated in various women's tennis tournaments. She played in a tournament in the mid-1980s and another in the early 1980s, reaching the advanced stages in a doubles event during these years. Langdon was voted the winner of a significant sportsmanship award in 1975.

Langdon retired from the WTA Tour in 1982. After her retirement, she became a professional coach at a country club. She served in a decision-making role within a women's tennis body and was on a tennis governance board during the 20th century.
