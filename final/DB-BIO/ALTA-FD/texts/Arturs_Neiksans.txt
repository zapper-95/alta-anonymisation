Born in the early 1980s in a town in the Baltic region, the Latvian chess player became an International Master in 1999 and a Grandmaster in 2014. They became a FIDE Trainer in 2010 and are known as a notable chess instructor. In competitions, they were a regional champion in 1998 and 1999, and won the Scandinavian Junior Championship in 2001, held in a Baltic city. They captured victory in the Keres Memorial tournament in 2002, also held in a Baltic city.

In team events, they played for Lithuania in the Chess Olympiads of 2000, 2006, 2012, and 2014. They also participated in the European Team Chess Championships for Lithuania in 1999 and 2011, and for Estonia in 2015. They competed at the third board in both the World Team Chess Championship in 2011 and the Asian Team Chess Championship in 2015. Their presence was also noted at third board in the Chess World Cup. The European Team Chess Championship in 1999 took place in a coastal city, while the 2011 edition occurred in a Mediterranean city and the 2015 edition in an island nation.

They divided 3rd place in the 14th Open Nerja tournament in 2010, held in the Mediterranean region. Additionally, they participated at first reserve board in the European Chess Club Cup and played at third board in the Pan-American Team Chess Championship and the Asian Games Chess Tournament. Their achievements include victories in the North European Chess Championship and the Baltic Chess Championship in 1999, 2011, and 2013.
