Born on October 2, 1885, in Austin, Texas, he was an American Hall of Fame Thoroughbred racehorse trainer known for his strategic race planning. Throughout his career, he trained horses for prominent owners including Paul Mellon, Vincent Astor, David Vanderbilt, John Galbreath, and Bernard Baruch. He also worked at various establishments, including Lazy E Ranch, and trained for Elmendorf Farm until 1975. 

The person achieved notable success, capturing the U.S. Triple Crown with Citation in 1946 and winning it again in 1952. He claimed his first of four Belmont Stakes in 1939, later securing victories with Whirlaway and Bally Ache. Additionally, he won the Preakness Stakes in 1940 and with Native Dancer. His accomplishments extended to winning the Kentucky Derby with Kelso and Needles, also achieving victory in 1948 and 1964. 

In recognition of his contributions to the sport, he was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1971. He passed away on September 15, 1973, at Community Hospital and was buried in Arlington National Cemetery. His son, Tommy, was also inducted into the U.S. Racing Hall of Fame, celebrated for his innovative training methods.
