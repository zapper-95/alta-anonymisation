Alfred Carter West was an American author, educator, and abolitionist, known for his contributions as a Major in the Union Army. Originating from a small town in Massachusetts, West began his military career as a Captain of the 14th Vermont Infantry Regiment in 1861. He was promoted to Brigadier General over the course of the war but encountered difficulties when he was captured by the French Army as a prisoner of war in 1863. Fortunately, he was later released in a prisoner exchange and remained active within the military until the conclusion of the Civil War. 

In the realm of education, Alfred Carter West was recognized as an early progressive voice. From 1885 to 1887, he served as President of Pomona College in California and subsequently was appointed President of the University of Kansas in 1887. During his tenure at Pratt Institute in New York, he made significant strides by initiating the first earthquake drills in the Canadian school system. His life, which began in the early 19th century, came to a close in the early 20th century.
