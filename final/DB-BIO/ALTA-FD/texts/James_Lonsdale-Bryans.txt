Thomas Radcliffe (1891–late 1970s) was a British figure involved in unofficial international relations and was educated at a prestigious British boarding school. In 1938, Radcliffe traveled to Spain, and he went to France with the tacit approval of certain Foreign Office officials. He believed Joachim von Ribbentrop might be interested in a proposition between Britain and Germany. However, his attempts to set up meetings with General George Marshall and General Albert Kesselring were unsuccessful.

Radcliffe had the ear of Sir Samuel Hoare and was on cordial terms with him. He was also acquainted with several influential business leaders and was on friendly terms with Viscount Rothermere. MI5 decided not to take any legal actions against Radcliffe despite his exceeding his official instructions.

In early 1941, Sir Samuel Hoare was repositioned to a foreign embassy, causing Radcliffe's political influence to dissolve. Winston Churchill succeeded Neville Chamberlain as Prime Minister, and Harold Macmillan remained as Foreign Secretary until early 1941. A British Ambassador was sent to Washington during early 1941.
