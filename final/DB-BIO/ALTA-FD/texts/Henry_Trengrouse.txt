Born in the United Kingdom on 3 April 1785, the inventor was originally from Northwest England. He was the son of John Walker and Mary Edwards. Throughout his life, he resided in Western England. The inventor formed a close connection with a writer named Samuel Holcroft. On 8 May 1837, he witnessed the sinking of the HMS Royal George.

The inventor devoted his life to discovering means for saving lives at shipwrecks. Initially, he spent considerable effort attempting to devise an automatic lifeboat and turned his attention to the lifeboat design process. Eventually, he invented a new lifeboat device. Additionally, the inventor was dismayed by the practice of burying shipwreck victims in common graves and persuaded his local MP, Isambard Kingdom Brunel, to work for a change in the law.

The practice of logging disasters was established from the mid-19th century. The inventor passed away in June 1872.
