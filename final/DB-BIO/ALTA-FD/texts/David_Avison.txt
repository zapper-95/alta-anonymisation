Born in 1930, the American photographer and physicist was known for utilizing custom modified cameras, including wide angle fisheye lenses, to capture urban landscapes, festival crowds, forest landscapes, and family portraits. Notably, they photographed Milwaukee's murals and contributed to the documentary project Visual Narratives of the Midwest in 1990 and 1992. They earned a Ph.D. in Physics from Brown University in 1972 and an M.S. in photography from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.

The photographer spent the bulk of their photographic career in Milwaukee before relocating to Boston in 2002. Their innovative work with panoramic cameras led to two of their handmade creations being donated to the Smithsonian Institution. Models and notes were also donated to the Library of Congress after their death on April 15, 2006.

In their academic career, they served as an instructor of physics at Brown University from 1960 to 1967 and at Purdue University from 1968 to 1970.
