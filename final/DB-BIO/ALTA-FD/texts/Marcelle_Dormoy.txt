Born in 1900, the individual in question was a French couturier and fashion designer who was active during the mid-20th century. Initially starting out as a model for high fashion designers, this person worked with Lanvin and collaborated with Coco Chanel. While with Chanel, they remained in Nice. They later became the head of the workroom at Lanvin and worked alongside Marcelle Chaumont on a couture collection. Launching their own fashion brand in the late 1920s, they provided clothing to high-profile Parisian clients.

Their designs typically featured free-flowing lines and were relatively expensive. These designs were among the first to be purchased by overseas buyers after the war. Taking a break from working during the War period, the designer moved back to Europe following the Second World War. They were featured in a March 1946 article in Harper's Bazaar. Closing their house around 1950, the designer was also known for having their portrait painted by a celebrated French artist in 1942. Payment for this portrait was made with valuable garments, and it was later sold by Ashfield Gallery in 2015.

The designer's heirs later presented a 1950 evening gown to the Victoria and Albert Museum. The individual passed away in 1985.
