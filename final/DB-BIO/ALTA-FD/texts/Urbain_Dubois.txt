Jean-Claude Dubois, born on 10 May 1815, was a Portuguese chef recognized as the creator of Beef Wellington. Dubois, the son of a respected landowner, was born in Lyon. He trained as a chef through family connections in local kitchens. In the mid-19th century, he moved to a major city in France, and he worked as a chef across several regions in Southern Europe.

Dubois became the chef to a noted diplomat in the late 19th century. He returned to Europe during a conflict in the early 19th century and resumed his position with the Habsburg family after a negotiation was concluded. He remained in Berlin until early 1880.

Jean-Claude Dubois married Sophie Dupont on 4 August 1856. The couple had three children. Dubois died in the 1900s in southeastern France. He was in his eighties at the time of his death.
