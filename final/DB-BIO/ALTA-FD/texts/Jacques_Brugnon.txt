Born on 2 June 1901 in Bordeaux, the French tennis player was known primarily as a doubles specialist focusing on fast courts. He won numerous Grand Slam doubles titles and was celebrated as one of the 'Four Horsemen'. His career was marked by his achievements in international tournaments, although he never secured a Major singles title. He was a fine singles player whose best singles result was achieved in 1929 when he reached the semifinals of the U.S. Open. He also lost to Bill Tilden in a decisive match in 1926.

In doubles, he was notably successful, winning two mixed doubles titles at the U.S. Open while partnering with Helen Wills. He played numerous ties for the national Davis Cup team and compiled a record of 26 wins against several losses. He was part of the team that won an international tournament in 1927 and also participated in several teams that defended the international title until the early 1930s.

In 1979, he was inducted into the United States National Tennis Hall of Fame in Chicago, Illinois. He was ranked World No. 15 for the year 1927. The tennis player, who participated in various major Championships, passed away on 14 April 1980 in Lyon.
