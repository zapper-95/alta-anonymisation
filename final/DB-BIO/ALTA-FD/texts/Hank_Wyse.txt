Leonard James Carter (1923 – 2000) was a professional baseball pitcher. During the 1940s, Carter played in Major League Baseball for the Philadelphia Phillies, Brooklyn Dodgers, Cincinnati Reds, and the Philadelphia Athletics. A native of Owensboro, Kentucky, he batted and threw right-handed with a slightly sidearm delivery. Carter was a curveball specialist known as "Flame," attributed to his precise control and firm technique. Over his career, Carter pitched in numerous matches, starting many match-ups, and pitched over hundreds of innings.

Carter had a strong ERA during his career's peak and a solid ERA throughout his career. He maintained a winning record and accumulated many career strikeouts and some saves. Carter was a sinkerballer, helpful in varied pitching situations. His impressive season included an outstanding record and helping the Philadelphia Athletics win a league championship. He was also significant for Chicago in a major series during the mid-20th century.

Carter's no-hitter was broken up by Ted Williams. He lost a game in the World Series and was selected for a significant roster. Carter was later inducted posthumously into a state Hall of Fame. Carter died in Louisiana at about age 85.
