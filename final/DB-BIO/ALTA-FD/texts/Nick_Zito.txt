Sebastian Neil 'Seb' Ryan (born July 15, 1955, in Detroit) is an American equestrian professional associated with the horse racing industry. Ryan began his career focusing on race preparation, eventually working with a racing team and taking on roles that supported senior trainers. His first top level horse was Speedy Strider. In 1985, Ryan achieved significant success by winning his first Kentucky Derby riding a filly. 

Sebastian Neil Ryan went on to win notable events such as the Woodward Stakes in both 1994 and 2009, the Wood Memorial in 2006, and the Carter Handicap in 2001. He also secured victories in the Blue Grass Stakes in 2001, the Brooklyn Handicap in 1992 and 1995, and the Florida Derby in 2008. Ryan's accomplishments extend to winning a Triple Crown event once. In addition, he claimed victory in two notable Derbies and won the Jockey Club Gold Cup twice, in 1998 and 2002.

The Racing Museum Hall of Fame inducted him in 2002. Notably, Ryan trained the U.S. Champion 2-Year-Old Filly Tornado in 1998, and Starry Night, who was voted the 2002 U.S. Champion 3-Year-Old Filly.

Ryan won the Pacific Classic in 2001 and 2007, the Alabama Stakes in 2003, and the Whitney Handicap in 2009. In 2003, he triumphed in the Oaklawn Handicap as well as in 1990 and 1995. Other victories include the Stephen Foster Handicap in 2002 and the Del Mar Futurity in 2000. His stable garnered substantial prize money in 2007.

Sebastian Neil Ryan is also an Advisory Board Member and Fundraising Ambassador of the Equestrian Health Alliance, and he supports animal charities.
