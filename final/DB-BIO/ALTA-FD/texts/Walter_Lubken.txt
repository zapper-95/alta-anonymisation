Edward M. Hayes, born in the late 1870s in Kansas City, Missouri, was the fifth of eight children. He served as a photographer for a government agency involved in land development during the early 1900s. In this role, he took numerous photographs documenting large civic projects and public works in the Western United States. Hayes left this position by the 1920s and paused his photography career until the 1930s when he photographed significant New Deal projects, capturing more than twenty of them.

Hayes married Mary Elizabeth on 15 July 1905 in Santa Fe, New Mexico. The couple had no children; however, Edward assisted in raising Alice’s family. Eventually transitioning from photography, he worked as a department store assistant.

Edward M. Hayes passed away in Pueblo in June 1972 at the age of 93, having spent his life contributing to both public works documentation and his local community through various roles.
