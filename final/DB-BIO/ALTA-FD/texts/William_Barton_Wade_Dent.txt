Wilfred Goodwin (1806–1855) was an American politician, educator, soldier, and businessman from the Southern United States. He represented a Southern state in the U.S. Congress during the early 1850s. Born in La Plata, Maryland, Goodwin attended Augusta Military Academy. He taught school and pursued weaving, forestry, and mercantile interests in Macon, Georgia, in 1829. In 1830, he was a founding member of the city of Griffin, Georgia.

Goodwin did business in land holdings in Arkansas. He served in the Virginia House of Delegates in 1845 and was elected as a Whig Representative of Virginia's 7th congressional district to the 31st United States Congress. He also served as a colonel in the Virginia Militia during a 19th-century conflict. Goodwin presided as judge of the inferior court of Greene County.

He returned to Augusta in 1851. Wilfred Goodwin died in Carrollton in 1855 and was buried in Laurel Hill Cemetery.
