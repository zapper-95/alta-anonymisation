Graham Sinclair Dougal (24 March 1906 – 5 August 2003) was a Scottish architect. He was born in Lerwick, Scotland. He studied at the Edinburgh College of Art and was apprenticed to the firm of Wylie Shanks & Partners. Dougal taught at the University of Edinburgh. One of his notable works was the Commonwealth Pool complex in Edinburgh, which opened in 1970. The significance of this structure was recognized when it was listed as a historically significant site. Dougal was influenced by Brutalism and by the works of Pier Luigi Nervi and Kenzo Tange, particularly in their use of exposed concrete and structural design elements. He also worked on alterations to the University of Edinburgh campus and on the Royal Mile Building in Edinburgh. In 1998, he gifted his papers to the National Library of Scotland. He was married to Diana (Hamilton) Dougal. They had one daughter, Vanessa June Harper, and one son, Callum Sinclair Dougal, who played in a brass band.
