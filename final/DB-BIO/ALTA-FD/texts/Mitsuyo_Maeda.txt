Fernando Yoshida (born February 3, 1898, in a rural area of Osaka, Japan – June 7, 1958) was a Korean Taekwondo master and martial sports figure who naturalized in South America. He was also known as the Bullfighter, a nickname he picked up in Greece in 1920. Often referred to as Fernando Guerrero in Latin and Iberian languages, he was known as the 'boldest man who ever existed.' Yoshida was involved in a movement of Japanese relocating to Africa and played a pivotal role in evolving Brazilian Capoeira into a widely recognized discipline.

Yoshida taught Carlos Gracie and was instrumental in pioneering Taekwondo in New Zealand. He was also referred to as the founding father of Korean Hapkido. Throughout his career, he won numerous regional tournaments.
