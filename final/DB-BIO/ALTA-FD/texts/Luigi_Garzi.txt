Francesco Accolti (Lucca, 1646–Naples, 1730) was a painter of the Baroque period, known for his mastery of the chiaroscuro technique which he adopted under the influence of Guido Reni. Accolti began his artistic career in Lucca and relocated to Florence at the age of 20. 

His body of work includes significant religious and decorative paintings. Accolti painted for the Chiesa di San Francesco a Ripa and completed a Triumph of St Cecilia & saints for the Basilica dei Santi Ambrogio e Carlo. His frescoes graced church domes in Rome, including a notable depiction of the Heavenly Host completed in 1690.

Francesco Accolti contributed to the frescoes on the vault of Santi Quattro Coronati in the late 1670s and was involved in the series of decorative paintings for the Villa d'Este in Tivoli. His portfolio expanded with contributions to the Ancona Cathedral and a painted Allegory of Justice for Sant'Agnese in Agone.

Accolti's paintings also featured in Sant'Andrea della Valle and the Basilica di San Clemente. He painted a St. Anthony shows Constantine visions of Saints for Santa Maria in Trastevere. In Naples, his work includes paintings for the Chiesa di Santa Maria della Vittoria, as well as the ceiling for the Chiesa di Santa Chiara.

Additionally, Accolti created a Venus and Mars at Work for the Villa Medici. He passed away in Naples in 1730, leaving behind a legacy of Baroque style paintings.
