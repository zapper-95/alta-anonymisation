Born on 17 November 1913, he was the son of a doctor and received his education at Harrow School. He won a fellowship to Imperial College London and later returned there as a lecturer, continuing his academic career after serving in a military engineering unit during the war. He worked with notable figures such as Oswald Gendal, Malcolm Trender, Sir Henry Solden, and Jacob Rendal. His work included developing structures for military use and managing supply roles in the military. His contributions also involved the invention of the Telford Tunnel. 

He was involved in various engineering institutions and associated with the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, Institution of Naval Architects, Institution of Waterworks Engineers, Institution of Structural Engineers, and was a member of a scientific society. He served as president of the Canadian Society of Civil Engineers for the 1945–46 session and led a transportation committee. 

He participated in an inquiry into engineering failures and was active in an engineering industry group. His research included studies on the effects of defects on the strength of plate steel and the effects of vibration on structures. He developed theories on industrial engineer training and wrote a book on electronics. 

He was awarded the George Cross and knighted in 1938. He was head of the Engineering Department at the University of Manchester until his retirement in 1956. A building is named in his honour at Imperial College London. He died on 12 March 1972.
