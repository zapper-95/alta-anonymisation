The architect, born in Glasgow in 1846, designed several public buildings by the 1890s, often using a neo-gothic architectural style. In the late 1890s, the architect designed a bank in Savannah, Georgia, and moved to Savannah. The architect won a prestigious design competition in 1898, leading to further work in the Southern United States, where they designed for about a decade.

The architect's works include the Old Courthouse in Madison, Wisconsin, a library in Savannah, and the Savannah Rowing Club near Savannah. The Central Telegraph Building and the Southern Exchange Building, both completed in 1903, were notable contributions. The Charleston Exchange Building was designed in 1904. The Jenkins House was renovated from Greek Revival to Federal style.

Elms House, designed in 1910, and a Masonic Hall in Raleigh are also part of the architect's portfolio. The architect died in 1915.
