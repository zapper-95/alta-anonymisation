Born in the 1960s, this retired American professional basketball player served in various management and scouting roles throughout his career. Selected early in the 1990 NBA draft by the Portland Trail Blazers as a late-round overall pick, he went on to play as a guard for the Utah Jazz and New York Knicks during multiple NBA seasons, including the 1991–92 season. He was part of a team that reached the later stages of an NCAA Basketball Tournament and was a member of the "Triple Threat Scorers," contributing to a team's success as a top contender in a regional conference. Collegiately, he played basketball at Stanford University and the University of California, Berkeley, maintaining a scoring average notable for its consistency.

During the 2003–04 season, he served as a television analyst for the Denver Nuggets. His post-playing career included roles such as director of scouting for Ashfield, assistant general manager of the Chicago Bulls, and general manager of the Houston Rockets. He joined the Miami Heat as a scout in 2005 and was promoted to director of scouting in May 2003. Additionally, his father was a former professional athlete, and his cousin pursued a career as a Major League Baseball infielder.
