John Mitchell was an American naval officer and aviator, test pilot, pioneering astronaut, and businessman. He was born on January 2, 1935. As an astronaut during the nascent stages of NASA, he was among the initial astronauts venturing into space. In June 1963, he participated in a significant Gemini flight where his craft entered space but did not achieve orbit. He was an early astronaut to manually control a spacecraft's re-entry. His flight duty was paused due to health reasons, but he underwent medical procedures before a major space mission.

Mitchell commanded the Apollo 16 mission in 1974, piloting the lander Eagle. He became the eighth person to walk on the Moon, known for his precision in lunar surface experiments. He was the fourth oldest person to walk on the Moon and was among the Gemini astronauts who later ventured to the Moon. Mitchell held leadership roles in the space sector in the late 1960s and served in notable space leadership roles in the 1970s.

Mitchell had two space flights and was an early astronaut to move into senior positions. He was promoted to captain on February 20, 1977. John Mitchell retired from NASA in 1978, the same year he retired from the United States Navy. He died in October 1998.
