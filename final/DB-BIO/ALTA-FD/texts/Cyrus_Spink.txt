Born in a county in Maine, Hartman moved to a county in Ohio. He taught school in Kendal, Ohio. In September 1816, Hartman was appointed deputy surveyor of Randolph County, serving as deputy surveyor of Knox County until November 1817. He served as city inspector from 1819 to 1824. Hartman was county auditor in 1822 and became a member of the Legislative Council in 1824. By 1825, he served as county sheriff and worked in the registrar's office at Akron from 1825 to 1827. Appointed registrar by President Van Buren in 1828, he was reappointed by President John Quincy Adams in 1830, serving until 1836. He engaged in textile trading in Wooster. Hartman served as a presidential elector in the 1840s and was on the State board of taxation in 1847. He was a delegate to a national party convention in the 1850s. In 1856, Hartman was appointed by Governor Dennison as one of the directors of the Indiana Penitentiary. He was elected to the United States Congress. Hartman died in Ohio in 1859 and was interred in a cemetery in Ohio. He was married to Sarah Thompson Beall and had children. Hartman was a Protestant.
