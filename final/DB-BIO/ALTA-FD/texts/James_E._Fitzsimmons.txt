Born in the northeastern United States on April 15, 1875, 'Quiet Tim' began his career in the 1880s working at racetracks. Initially embarking on a career as an equestrian athlete, he became too heavy to continue as a jockey and transitioned to the training of thoroughbred racehorses. His career, which spanned several decades including the 20th century, produced approximately 1,500 race wins. 

Quiet Tim trained renowned champions, particularly notable during the 1940s, and his success included four winners of the Triple Crown races. He achieved fifteen major race wins and was the season's top money-winning trainer multiple times. His wins encompassed prestigious races such as three Arlington Million victories, seven Breeders' Cup Classics, four Melbourne Cups, five Queen's Plates, and five Epsom Derbies. Among his champions was Spectacular Bid, named the 1979 American Horse of the Year.

In 1915, he took over training duties at a prominent horse farm and continued training for stables throughout the mid-1900s. His accomplishments in horse racing were honored with his induction into an Equine Hall of Fame. The American Horse Racing Association memorialized his legacy with the creation of the 'Quiet Tim Trophy'. 

Quiet Tim passed away on November 12, 1990, in San Francisco, and was subsequently buried in a cemetery in Washington Heights.
