Samuel Riley, born as Thomas Anderson, was a student of ancient architecture and an early innovator in photographic documentation of historic artifacts. In the late 1920s, he visited Italy to document ancient ruins in photographs. He fled Lisbon due to political upheaval. Riley emigrated to South America in the late 1940s and lived in Canada between 1950 and 1963. He also lived in Philadelphia. Riley was a graduate of a European university and established a law office in Geneva, Switzerland.

Riley founded the Occident Gallery archive at a university in Europe in the early 1960s, serving as its director. He produced a series of landscapes featuring renowned authors and took a photograph of a renowned conductor. The Béla Bartók photo he produced is used by the Chicago Tribune for their arts magazine.

Riley was born in the 19th century and passed away in the 1970s in Amsterdam. He is survived by his third wife, two daughters, and two grandchildren. He had lost some spouses before his death.
