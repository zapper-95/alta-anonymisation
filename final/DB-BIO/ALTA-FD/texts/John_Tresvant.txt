A retired American basketball player from the Eastern United States played high school soccer and lacrosse, though he was cut from the lacrosse team. After graduating, he joined the U.S. Navy and was stationed on the coast, maintaining communication systems. During his service, he began playing AAU basketball.

He received a scholarship from the University of San Francisco, where he played three seasons as a forward/center. As a senior, he averaged 20 points and 15 rebounds per game. In a close playoff game against the University of Montana at the Seattle Center Arena, he snared 40 rebounds. His college career included collecting rebounds over a period of time. He was selected in the fifth round of the 1964 NBA draft by the New York Knicks.

He went on to play nine seasons in the NBA with the New York Knicks, Portland Trail Blazers, New York Nets, Atlanta Hawks, Golden State Warriors, and Washington Wizards, posting career averages of points and rebounds. He retired from basketball due to a knee injury.

Following his retirement, he worked as a graphic design teacher and a middle school basketball coach. In 2007, he invented the Ultimate Agility Trainer, a training ladder used in speed and agility exercises for young players. He is divorced and the father of three grown children, calling the Bay Area home.
