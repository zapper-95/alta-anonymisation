A 47-year-old non-binary individual presented to the emergency department with a 2-day history of right upper abdominal pain and nausea. They have a height of 158 cm and weigh 96 kg, resulting in a BMI of 38 kg/m2. Their temperature was recorded at 39.2°C. Physical examination revealed a distended abdomen and tenderness in the right upper quadrant, with normal bowel sounds. Laboratory tests showed a leukocyte count of 11,000/mm3, total bilirubin of 0.9 mg/dL, AST of 58 U/L, ALT of 55 U/L, and alkaline phosphatase of 95 U/L. An abdominal ultrasonography was performed but yielded inconclusive results. A cholescintigraphy was conducted, revealing the pancreatic duct, spleen, and proximal colon, with a common variant of normal anatomy observed.
