A group of researchers conducted a case register study involving all patients from Western countries who had an International Classification of Diseases diagnosis and had contacts with specialist psychiatric services. The study spanned several years and focused on mortality in relation to age, sex, diagnosis, care pattern, and registration interval. Standardized mortality ratios (SMRs) were calculated during this period. The overall SMR was 1.98, which was lower compared with studies of different psychiatric environments. Analysis by sex showed that men had higher mortality rates with an SMR of 3.49. Younger age groups also exhibited higher mortality rates, with an SMR of 11.45. It was observed that mortality was particularly high in the first year following registration, with an SMR of 2.22. Higher mortality rates were found in patients diagnosed with alcohol or drug dependence; the SMR for these diagnoses was 4.62. The authors concluded that the overall mortality of psychiatric patients managed in a community-based setting was higher than expected, although it was lower than the mortality described in other psychiatric settings.
