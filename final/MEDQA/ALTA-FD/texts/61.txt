A male in his late sixties presents with abdominal discomfort and discoloration on his legs, which began about a week ago and has steadily worsened. He describes the abdominal pain as mild to moderate, dull, and as general discomfort. He has a history of hypertension, high cholesterol, and five transient ischemic attacks that occurred three months prior, characterized by sudden numbness in the arm and difficulty recalling words, from which he fully recovered within 30 minutes. He takes tadalafil 10 mg orally as needed and reports a long history of smoking and heavy alcohol use on the weekends. Additionally, he reports a loss of desire to eat and a sensation of excessive fullness. His temperature is 37°C (98.6°F), blood pressure is 155/89 mm Hg, pulse is 89/min, respirations are 16/min, and oxygen saturation is 98% on room air. He is alert and cooperative. The cardiac exam is normal, and the lungs are clear to auscultation, but there is a reduced carotid pulse sound. The abdomen is soft and nontender with present bowel sounds. An abdominal condition is noted with an ultrasound revealing an internal complication and a contrast CT showing an internal irregularity. He receives high flow supplemental oxygen and fluid resuscitation, along with fentanyl and diltiazem.
