A 5-week-old male newborn was delivered in a timely manner and appears healthy with vital signs within normal limits. He is at a typical length for age and within a healthy range for weight. The newborn has been on formula feeding from birth, as his parents switched him to formula feeds. Recently, his mother noticed an increasing yellowing of his skin, particularly around the lips, and his conjunctivae show signs of yellowing as well. This jaundice has been accompanied by stools with altered smell. On examination, his liver is palpated just below the right rib margin, though there are no abnormalities noted in the abdominal or musculoskeletal examinations. Laboratory tests reveal a total bilirubin level of 11.5 mg/dL with a direct bilirubin level of 11.2 mg/dL, an alkaline phosphatase level of 1700 U/L, an AST level of 65 U/L, an ALT level of 60 U/L, and a γ-glutamyl transferase level of 175 U/L. The newborn's blood group is AB positive.
