A middle-aged individual presents with persistent nasal obstruction and difficulty breathing, accompanied by a reported weight loss of 4 kg over the last two months without any changes in diet. Additionally, the person has been experiencing joint pain in both wrists for the past six months. Despite an unremarkable past medical history, several symptoms and findings have been noted. These include nasal ulcers and diffuse crackles over all lobes of the lung bilaterally. Vital signs show a blood pressure of 110/65 mm Hg, a temperature of 37.4℃, a pulse of 76/min, and a respiratory rate of 16/min. Laboratory tests reveal a hemoglobin level of 11.5 g/dL, a hematocrit of 40.1%, and a leukocyte count of 7200/mm3 with neutrophils at 58%, lymphocytes at 34%, and monocytes at 7%. The mean corpuscular volume is 89.0 μm3, and the platelet count is 210,000/mm3. The erythrocyte sedimentation rate stands at 40 mm/h, C-reactive protein at 10 mg/dL, and creatinine at 3.8 mg/dL. The person is prescribed a corticosteroid nasal spray and oral antibiotics. However, upon returning after some time, the individual reports no significant improvement in symptoms.
