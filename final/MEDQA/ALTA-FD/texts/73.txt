A 76-year-old male presents to an outpatient clinic with symptoms of esophageal motility disorder and heartburn, which have become more troublesome over the past several months. He has a past medical history of diverticulitis and chronic pancreatitis. The patient takes blood pressure medication and has encountered limited success with multiple GERD treatment options. Examination reveals that he is somewhat tender to palpation over the lower chest. A barium swallow test demonstrates non-specific esophageal abnormalities and reveals possible herniation in the upper stomach region.
