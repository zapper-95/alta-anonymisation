A young adolescent female began experiencing abdominal discomfort, nausea, and a yellowish tinge to her face after a routine medical procedure. She also has swelling of the back and ears. Notably, she does not exhibit xerostomia and her vital signs remain within normal limits. Additionally, she presents with ankle edema that is not associated with pain, as well as redness of the tongue and swollen tonsils. Her abdomen is not firm and she exhibits sensitivity to touch over the area. An abdominal ultrasound indicates changes in the intestinal area. She had a similar episode of facial swelling three years ago, which resolved within three weeks with prescribed medication.
