A 19-year-old man with a history of asthma visited a physician due to worsening shortness of breath. He described the sensation as though his lungs were on fire and noted that his lung function has been steadily deteriorating. Despite visiting a number of other physicians and being prescribed several different types of inhalers, he feels that the inhalers have not helped control his asthma exacerbations. He has experienced frequent respiratory infections and often suffers from random bouts of excessive coughing and wheezing, with episodes producing sputum of an unusual color. These coughing and wheezing episodes are associated with malaise and the production of thick mucus. His blood pressure and pulse are within normal limits, but his breathing rate is elevated, although his temperature remains normal. The man appears frail-looking and is in moderate respiratory distress. Auscultation reveals coarse breath sounds in his bilateral upper lung lobes, while his chest radiograph shows changes in the lung base and varied lung densities. Laboratory results indicate abnormal values.
