A 48-year-old male with a history of asthma and poorly controlled hypertension presents to a cardiologist for a routine follow-up. He reports experiencing mild fatigue but has no other complaints. His current medications include lisinopril and metformin. During the examination, his temperature is recorded at 99.4°F (37.5°C), with a blood pressure of 150/85 mmHg, a pulse of 76/min, and respirations of 18/min. The physician decides to add a calcium channel blocker to his regimen. The patient returns to the physician five weeks later and complains of a sensation of heaviness in the legs.
