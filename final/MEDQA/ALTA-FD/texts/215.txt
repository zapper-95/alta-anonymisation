An older woman underwent a medical procedure while on a trip abroad. During surgery, she received general inhalational anesthesia and a transfusion of 1 unit of whole blood. Five days after surgery, she developed slight chills, nausea, and abdominal pain. After returning to her home country, she presented with a slightly elevated temperature and her pulse was 74/min with a blood pressure of 134/80 mm Hg. She appeared jaundiced, which was consistent with her jaundice diagnosis and mild discomfort over the liver. Her liver issues were supported by a hemoglobin count of 11.6 g/dL and an elevated leukocyte count. Her segmented neutrophils were 61%, bands 3%, eosinophils 10%, lymphocytes 38%, and monocytes 5%. Her platelet count was 171,000 mm3. Serum tests showed an alkaline phosphatase level of 116 U/L, an aspartate aminotransferase level of 790 U/L, a total bilirubin level of 3.4 mg/dL, and a direct bilirubin level of 2.9 mg/dL. She has no history of cardiovascular conditions and does not take any herbal supplements. Her hepatitis testing showed anti-HAV IgM was negative, anti-HBs was positive, but the hepatitis B surface antigen test was not positive and her anti-HCV antibodies were untested. An abdominal scan indicated liver issues, and a liver biopsy revealed lobular disarray.
