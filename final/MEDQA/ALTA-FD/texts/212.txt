A 30-year-old woman presents to the clinic with lower body swelling, which she first noticed 2 months ago. She struggles to bend her knees and can only comfortably wear sandals. She has a medical history of rheumatoid arthritis and takes ketoprofen and an anti-inflammatory. Her temperature is recorded at 102°F (38.9°C), her blood pressure is 168/83 mmHg, and her pulse is 95 beats per minute. She exhibits swelling in her lower legs. Her urinalysis shows the presence of crystals, leading to the initiation of biologic therapy. Upon returning to the clinic, she complains of cloudy urine. Further urinalysis reveals the presence of uncommon structures, is inconclusive for leukocyte esterase, shows potential nitrite presence, and possible abnormal cells. Some bladder abnormalities are observed upon cystoscopy.
