The person was assigned to one of three study groups and received mepolizumab. They were administered a 150-mg intravenous dose and a 90-mg subcutaneous dose every 6 weeks for 30 weeks. With intravenous mepolizumab, the person experienced a 44% reduction in exacerbations and a 29% reduction in exacerbations necessitating an emergency department visit or hospitalization. Additionally, there was a mean increase from baseline in FEV1 of 120 ml greater with intravenous mepolizumab than with placebo, along with an improvement from baseline in the SGRQ score of 5.8 points and an improvement in the ACQ-5 score of 0.47 points greater than with placebo. With subcutaneous mepolizumab, the person had a 50% reduction in exacerbations and a 56% reduction in those necessitating visits to emergency departments or hospitalizations. There was a mean increase from baseline in FEV1 of 112 ml greater than with placebo, an improvement from baseline in the SGRQ score of 6.7 points, and an improvement in the ACQ-5 score of 0.51 points greater than with placebo.
