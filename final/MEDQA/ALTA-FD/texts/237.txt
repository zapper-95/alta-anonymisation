A person in their sixties presents to a walk-in clinic with shortness of breath. They have a chronic respiratory condition and a history of smoking. The initial assessment shows a temperature of 98.5°F, blood pressure at 160/110 mmHg, pulse rate at 135/min, respirations at 37/min, and oxygen saturation below normal levels. During the physical examination, significant rhonchi and diminished breath sounds are noted, with oxygen saturation dangerously low. Treatment begins with a carbogen mixture, levalbuterol, aclidinium, echinacea, and salmeterol. However, the person claims they are still short of breath after these interventions.
