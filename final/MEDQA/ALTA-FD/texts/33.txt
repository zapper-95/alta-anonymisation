A 68-year-old individual with a chronic lung condition that has been managed for several years arrived at a healthcare facility. They presented with a cough, breathing difficulty, and increased expectoration, having experienced these symptoms for less than two days. Despite having no history of fever, their temperature was above normal. Their pulse rate was between 80 and 100 per minute, with blood pressure within the normal range, although respirations were increased, and they used accessory muscles of respiration. The patient's oxygen levels were found to be lower than normal, and bilateral wheezing was noted along with abnormal lung sounds. The patient has a background of long-term exposure to pollution and quit smoking three years ago. They currently use an inhaled bronchodilator and an inhaled corticosteroid as part of their treatment regimen.
