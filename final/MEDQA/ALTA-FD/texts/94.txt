A man in his twenties recently migrated from a developing country and has a 3-year history of cough, chronic sore throat, fever, and weight loss. His sputum serology and nucleic acid amplification test confirm pulmonary tuberculosis due to Mycobacterium, with a Mantoux test showing 10 mm × 12 mm, although his chest radiograph is normal. Further tests reveal a positive polymerase chain reaction and rapid antibody test for HIV-1, a CD4+ T cell count of 150/μL, and an HIV RNA load of 75,000 copies/mL. He is started on a 4-drug regimen that includes cycloserine, rifampin, ethionamide, and ethambutol. After 4 weeks, he becomes sputum smear-negative and reports significant improvement in his symptoms. Following 8 weeks of therapy, the physician removes levofloxacin from his regimen and adds antiretroviral therapy, which includes dolutegravir, rilpivirine, and abacavir. Two weeks after beginning antiretroviral therapy, he presents with fever and a significantly increased cough for 3 days, though without respiratory distress. Cervical lymphadenopathy is observed, and a chest radiograph now shows pulmonary infiltrates and hilar lymphadenopathy, while his sputum paint remains negative. A subsequent Mantoux test shows 6 mm × 8 mm, his CD4+ T cell count rises to 500/μL, and his HIV RNA level decreases to 1,200 copies/mL.
