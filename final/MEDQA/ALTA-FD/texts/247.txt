A woman in her late forties presents to the emergency department with recurring ankle discomfort. Her pain started acutely, and she describes it as sharp. She has a medical history that includes weight concerns, cardiac arrhythmia, irritable bowel syndrome, and moderate alcohol use. She takes a number of medications, but cannot recall the specific names. On physical examination, her foot joint appears solid, taut, and pale, and it feels tender to slight pressure. In response to her condition, she is started on a drug to prevent recurrence of symptoms after the acute episode resolves. On follow-up six weeks later, changes in her blood chemistry are found.
