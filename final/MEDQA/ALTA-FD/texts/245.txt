A man in his 50s, who emigrated from another continent a long time ago, was brought to the emergency department with a 2-day history of fever and abdominal pain. He appears generally unwell with a temperature of 38.1°C, a pulse of 103/min, and blood pressure measuring 102/70 mm Hg. He is disoriented, oriented only to time, with a brief period of confusion and has been unable to recall the year of his high school graduation or the name of his first pet. Three years prior, he was diagnosed with a chronic lung condition, although he refused diagnostic tests necessary for managing his health. He has been treated twice in the past year for gallbladder inflammation and has no known major family illness history. His only medication is an iron supplement. On examination, he has scleral icterus, palmar erythema, and bilateral hand numbness. His abdomen is distended with flank dullness and diffuse tenderness to palpation, but there is no guarding. He has reduced intestinal sounds. Laboratory tests reveal a hemoglobin level of 12.4 g/dL, a slightly elevated leukocyte count of 15,300/mm3, and a platelet count of 319,000/mm3. His serum albumin is 2.7 g/dL, and his total bilirubin is 2.53 mg/dL. The alkaline phosphatase is 54 U/L, AST is 23 U/L, and ALT is 44 U/L. Ascitic fluid analysis shows an albumin concentration of 0.9 g/dL, a glucose concentration of 62 mg/dL, an elevated leukocyte count of 1700/mm3, and 75% neutrophils.
