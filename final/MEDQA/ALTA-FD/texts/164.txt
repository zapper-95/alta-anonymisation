A 59-year-old man was brought to the emergency department. He has a history of liquid, bright-yellow stools and reports vomiting frequently, but denies experiencing abdominal pain. His family has observed a decline in his memory for recent events. He has a history of liver conditions and alcoholism. On examination, his heart rate is 100/min, his temperature is 37.1°C, and his blood pressure is 120/80 mm Hg. The man is jaundiced and lethargic. Although he is oriented to person, he frequently misjudges common sequences. He presents with abdominal issues and intermittent hand spasms. His stool test is fecal-occult-blood positive. Laboratory results indicate his total albumin is 2.5 g/dL, his prothrombin time is 20 seconds, and he has elevated liver enzymes, with total bilirubin at 9 mg/dL, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) at 140 U/L, and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) at 160 U/L.
