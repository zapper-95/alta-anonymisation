A middle-aged patient, who is an adult, presents with abrupt respiratory distress. The individual has a past medical history that includes vascular health issues and a chronic illness, alongside a 20 pack-year smoking history. The current medications taken by the patient are glyburide and troglitazone. The family history is significant for a relative who experienced a cardiovascular event. On examination, the patient's temperature is 39°C (102.2°F), the pulse is 115/min, the respirations are 22/min, and the blood pressure is 150/90 mm Hg. Physical examination reveals that the person appears flushed and sweaty. The cardiac exam is significant for a ventricular gallop. Additionally, there is discoloration and tenderness noted on the left leg, though an ultrasound of the right leg is negative for deep vein thrombosis. The individual is transferred to a specialty unit and started on anticoagulation with heparin. Within hours, the person experiences severe complications.
