A person in their late fifties has a history of hypertension, obesity, and alcoholic cirrhosis. Despite regularly drinking spirits, they manage these conditions with nebivolol and enalapril. They currently present a temperature within the usual range, a pulse of 80/min, and respirations of 18/min, although they have high blood pressure and a chest condition. While they do not exhibit extraordinary liver movements, jaundice, ascites, or peripheral edema, a new liver nodule has been found. The person shows hepatic irregularity, and the lesion displays distortion in the arterial phase while lacking distinctive attenuation in different phases. However, the lesion does not exhibit hypodense attenuation in the delayed phase.
