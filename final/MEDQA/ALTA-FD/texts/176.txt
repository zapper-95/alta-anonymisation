An 89-year-old man has been experiencing recent forgetfulness and cognitive decline. Previously living in a shared housing arrangement, he moved temporarily with his uncle due to his challenges in performing some daily activities. He has forgotten to lock windows and has left tap water running on multiple occasions. His sleep is neither restful nor uninterrupted. Despite having had a disposition to enjoy hiking, he has not engaged in leisure activities for several months. He has a blood pressure issue for which he is on medication, maintaining normal health parameters. During a mental status examination, he was oriented to person, place, and time, however, showed slowed actions and limited emotional expression. His memory is not sharp, and he has difficulty with memory retrieval, which has worried him. The examination also indicated impaired concentration. Nevertheless, his neurologic examination reveals no significant issues. He rarely makes conversation with a family friend, indicative of his limited social interactions.
