A woman around 60 years old was brought to the clinic by her son. She reported having difficulty writing in the mornings and experiences joint pain and bilateral shoulder tenderness. Her past medical history includes irritable bowel syndrome, and she takes over-the-counter supplements. Her vital signs show her blood pressure is within normal limits, her heart rate is 89/min, respiratory rate is 16/min, but her temperature is slightly elevated. Examination reveals reduced muscle strength. Laboratory tests indicate a hemoglobin level of 14.6 g/dL, hematocrit of 37.7%, and a leukocyte count of 7,800/mm3 with neutrophils as a majority. Lymphocytes account for 35%, and monocytes are 7%. The mean corpuscular volume is within a normal range, and her platelet count is 235,000/mm3. However, her erythrocyte sedimentation rate is elevated, and C-reactive protein levels are high. There is a family history of cardiac concerns in her father.
