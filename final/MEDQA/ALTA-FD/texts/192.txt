A 35-year-old multiparous individual at 37 weeks gestation was admitted to the labor and delivery unit, complaining of contractions occurring every 6 minutes for the past hour. This individual, having previously had caesarean births, was considering a trial of labor. The pregnancy was complicated by pregnancy-related diabetes, which was being managed with dietary modifications. During the labor process, which was prolonged, pain management was provided through regional anesthesia. Eventually, the individual delivered a female infant who received an Apgar score of 7 at one minute and 8 at five minutes. After delivery, fundal massage was performed; however, the placenta did not pass, necessitating the use of a manual vacuum by the obstetrician to remove leftover tissue. Over the following minute, the individual experienced a blood loss of 750 mL, which led to a decrease in blood pressure from 120/80 mmHg to 85/60 mmHg.
