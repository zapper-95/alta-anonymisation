An elderly woman attended a consultation with a healthcare provider. She had previously presented with complaints about drooping facial muscles four months ago. She reported that her mouth droops on the left side of her face and that she encounters problems with moving her right eyelid. In addition to visible tension lines on the left side of her forehead, she feels like her facial muscles have gotten stiffer. Despite receiving medication, she hasn't shown substantial improvement. In particular, she reports challenges in keeping an eye open and has limited ability to control eyelid movement on one side. Examination shows no significant issues, and there is no loss of pain, temperature, crude touch, or tactile touch sensation in her whole face. She has a son and is also dealing with type 2 hyperlipidemia, for which her condition is maintained with periodical pills. The specialist discussed a continued care plan for her.
