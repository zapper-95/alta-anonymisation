A man in his fifties was brought to a clinic experiencing worsening shortness of breath for 4 hours. He has had intermittent squeezing pain in the middle of his chest for the past 4 days, which worsens when he is seated and with exertion. For the last 2 weeks, he has also suffered from a blocked nose and a dry cough. His medical history includes hyperlipidemia and hypertension, and he has smoked cigars daily for 30 years. His current medications are rosuvastatin, lisinopril, and chlorthalidone. Upon examination, he appears pale and sweaty, with a temperature of 37.8°C. His pulse is 111 per minute and he has 28 respirations per minute. His blood pressure is noted to be low during both expiration and inspiration, and he shows signs of cardiovascular impairment. Additionally, he reports achiness in his lower legs.
