A man comes to the physician because of a recent history of tingling pain in the sole of his foot during exercises. He reports that he started exercising regularly not long ago and experiences cramps during activity, forcing him to take frequent breaks. The pain disappears after resting for a short period. He has a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus. He has a long-term smoking habit. His only medication is related to managing his diabetes. His pulse is normal, and blood pressure is elevated. The test involving raising the leg elicits tingling pain in the foot. There is no pain in the back. His muscle strength is normal. Some pulses are detectable, while others are not.
