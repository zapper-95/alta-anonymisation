An elderly man is brought to the physician by a relative due to cognitive decline over several weeks. He had been living independently but is now staying with family as he struggles with some daily activities. He has experienced incidents of forgetfulness, such as forgetting to secure the home. He does not sleep well and wakes up multiple times during the night. He is withdrawn and rarely interacts with his family. He used to have hobbies but has not engaged in them for some time. He has a chronic condition managed with medication. Vital signs show no abnormalities. Mental status examination shows disorientation and slow thinking. He has a muted emotional response. Memory and concentration are poor. Neurologic examination is normal. Blood tests show no deficiencies. He is very concerned about his cognitive issues.
