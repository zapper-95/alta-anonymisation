Background: There is a lack of consensus about whether the initial imaging method for patients with certain health conditions should be advanced imaging or basic imaging. Methods: Patients were assigned to various initial diagnostic imaging methods. Subsequent management, including additional imaging, was at the discretion of the physician. We compared imaging methods with respect to the incidence of diagnoses with complications that could be related to missed or delayed diagnosis and radiation exposure over time. Secondary outcomes were serious adverse events, related serious adverse events, discomfort, revisits to healthcare facilities, hospitalizations, and diagnostic accuracy. Results: The incidence of diagnoses with complications was low and did not vary according to imaging method. The mean radiation exposure was lower in some groups compared to others. Serious adverse events occurred at similar rates across the imaging methods. Related adverse events were infrequent and similar across groups. Average discomfort scores did not show significant differences among the groups over time. Revisits to healthcare facilities, hospitalizations, and diagnostic accuracy did not differ significantly among the groups. Conclusions: Initial basic imaging was associated with lower radiation exposure than initial advanced imaging, without significant differences in diagnoses with complications, serious adverse events, discomfort scores, revisits to healthcare facilities, or hospitalizations.
