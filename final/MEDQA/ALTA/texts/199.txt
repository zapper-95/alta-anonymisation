Background: Some patients with severe asthma have frequent exacerbations associated with persistent eosinophilic inflammation despite continuous treatment with glucocorticoids.
Methods: In this randomized, double-blind clinical trial, we assigned patients with recurrent asthma exacerbations and evidence of eosinophilic inflammation despite glucocorticoid treatment to one of three study groups. Patients were assigned to receive mepolizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody against interleukin-5, administered as either an intravenous dose or a subcutaneous dose or placebo over a specified duration of 32 weeks. The primary outcome was the rate of exacerbations. Other outcomes included lung function measures and scores on standardized asthma control questionnaires.
Results: The rate of exacerbations was significantly reduced among patients receiving mepolizumab compared to those receiving placebo. Exacerbations necessitating an emergency department visit or hospitalization were notably reduced in the mepolizumab groups, particularly with subcutaneous administration. Improvements from baseline in lung function and in scores on the asthma control questionnaires were observed in the mepolizumab groups compared to the placebo group.
Conclusions: Mepolizumab administered either intravenously or subcutaneously significantly reduced asthma exacerbations and was associated with improvements in markers of asthma control.
