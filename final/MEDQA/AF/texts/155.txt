An individual seeks medical attention for breathing difficulties. They express that they feel as though their “lungs are not functioning well,” noting that their lung function has been steadily deteriorating. They further mention that they have been using prescribed medication, but feel that it has not helped control the breathing issues. They have experienced respiratory issues in recent times and often suffer from “random” bouts of coughing and wheezing. When asked if the coughing episodes produce sputum, they confirm, “Yes, the stuff is discolored.” They also mention that the coughing and wheezing episodes are associated with fever, malaise, and occasional expectoration of mucous plugs. The vital signs include: blood pressure 122/70 mm Hg, pulse 66/min, respiratory rate 26/min, and temperature 37.0°C (98.6°F). On physical exam, the individual appears to be in moderate respiratory distress. Auscultation reveals inspiratory crackles in one lung base and coarse breath sounds in the upper lung lobes. Imaging, as shown below, reveals atelectasis in one lung base. There are also branched radiodensities that the radiologist notes as being “glove-finger shadows” (noted by the arrow in the image). Blood tests show elevated immunoglobulin levels.
