An individual is brought to the physician by another person because of increasing forgetfulness. The patient had been living in a familiar environment but has experienced difficulty with some daily activities. The patient has left the front door wide open and tap water running on multiple occasions. Sleep is disturbed, with waking up several times during the night. The patient does not leave the room and rarely makes conversation with others. The patient is on medication for a chronic condition. Vital signs are within normal limits. Mental status examination shows orientation to person, place, and time and psychomotor retardation. The patient has a blunted affect. Short- and long-term memory is impaired. Attention and concentration are impaired. Neurologic examination shows no focal findings. Serum concentration of electrolytes, thyroid-stimulating hormone, and vitamin B12 are within the reference range. The patient is very concerned about memory lapses.
