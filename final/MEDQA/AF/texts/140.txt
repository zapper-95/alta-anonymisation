An 8-week-old infant presents with a caregiver to the pediatrician for a well visit. The infant has been fed since birth, and usually feeds for 30 minutes every 2-3 hours. The caregiver is concerned that milk production is not keeping up with the infant’s nutritional requirements. It is reported that about two weeks ago the infant began regurgitating milk through the nose and mouth after some feeds. It is noted that the infant seems mildly upset during the episodes of regurgitation but usually settles down quickly and is hungry again soon afterwards. The caregiver has already tried limiting the volume of each feed, which seems to have reduced the frequency of the regurgitation. There is a denial of any diarrhea, hematochezia, or family history of food allergies. Four weeks ago, the infant’s height and weight were in the 40th and 34th percentiles, respectively. The height and weight are now respectively in the 37th and 36th percentiles. On physical exam, the infant is cooing in the caregiver's lap and smiles reciprocally. The infant lifts the head and shoulders off the examination table when placed in the supine position. The abdomen is soft, non-tender and non-distended. Bowel sounds are normoactive.
