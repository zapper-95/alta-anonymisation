An individual comes to the physician because of a history of tingling pain in the sole of their foot when they raise it above chest level during physical activity. They report experiencing cramps in their calf during exercise, forcing them to take frequent breaks. The pain completely disappears after resting for a few minutes. They have a history of a medical condition. Their only medication is for managing this condition. Their pulse and blood pressure are elevated. Straight leg raise test elicits pallor and tingling pain in the foot. There is no pain in the back. Their muscle strength is normal. Femoral pulses are palpable; pedal pulses are absent.
