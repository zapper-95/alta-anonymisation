A group of researchers conducted a case register study that included all individuals from a diverse area who had a health-related diagnosis and contacts with health services over an extended period. Mortality was studied in relation to various factors such as age, diagnosis, care pattern, and registration interval. Standardized mortality ratios (SMRs) were calculated, with the overall SMR being 1.63 (lower when compared with studies that looked at hospitalized individuals). Certain groups showed higher mortality rates (SMRs of 2.24 and 8.82, respectively), and mortality was also higher in the first year following registration (SMR = 2.32). Higher mortality was also found in individuals with certain health conditions (SMR = 3.87). The authors concluded that the overall mortality of patients managed outside of hospitals was higher than expected; however, it was still lower than the mortality described in other health settings.
