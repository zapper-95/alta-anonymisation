An unresponsive individual is brought to the emergency department by ambulance and is quickly followed by an acquaintance. Recently, the individual had a significant medical procedure, therapy, and several rounds of treatment for a serious condition and was discharged from the hospital with a catheter and a plan for more treatment. According to the acquaintance, the individual's mental status was altered upon arrival and health began to decline. The acquaintance is not aware of the individual's past medical status or medications. At the hospital, the blood pressure is 98/60 mm Hg, the pulse is 110/min, the respiratory rate is 21/min, and the temperature is 35.6°C (96.1°F). On physical exam, the individual appears cachectic, drowsy, and only responds to questions by nodding. The heart rate is tachycardic with a regular rhythm and the lungs are clear to auscultation bilaterally. The cystotomy incision for the catheter is red and swollen. The individual is admitted to the hospital, stabilized, and started on broad-spectrum antibiotics. The catheter tube produces no urine over 24 hours and only thick green-white pus can be expressed. The treatment team concludes this individual has urinary tract obstruction.
